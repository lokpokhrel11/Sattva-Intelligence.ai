Research Guide

Sanskrit Terms

A contextual guide to significant Sanskrit terms encountered across the Sattva Intelligence projects. Rather than assigning each term a single universal definition, this resource follows how terms function within particular texts and philosophical traditions.

Entries will expand as the Bhagavata Purana series and future work on the Bhagavad Gita, Yoga Sutra, Nyaya, and related traditions develop.

How to Use This Guide

Sanskrit terms often carry different meanings, emphases, or technical functions across texts and traditions. Each entry therefore begins with a basic semantic orientation and then identifies relevant textual contexts. Similar vocabulary across traditions should not be assumed to represent identical concepts.

This guide is selective rather than exhaustive. Terms are included when they receive substantive attention in Sattva Intelligence episodes, essays, or research materials. Additional textual contexts will be added as the project develops.

For Episodes 1–7, video links lead to the complete episode. From Episode 8 onward, precise timestamps are provided where the relevant discussion has been indexed.

A

Āśraya (आश्रय)

Basic sense: support, resort, substratum, or ground. The appropriate English rendering depends on context.

Bhagavata Purana: Āśraya is the tenth of the ten characteristics described in Canto 2, Chapter 10. The first nine characteristics are presented as contributing to an understanding of this tenth principle.

Avatāra (अवतार)

Basic sense: descent; in theological contexts, divine descent or manifestation.

Bhagavata Purana: The Bhagavata presents numerous forms of divine intervention. Canto 2, Chapter 7 recounts a sequence of such manifestations.

B

Bhakti (भक्ति)

Basic sense: devotion, participation, attachment, or devoted relationship, depending on context.

Bhagavata Purana: Bhakti is central to the Bhagavata's account of religious life. In Canto 1, Chapter 2, the highest dharma is associated with bhakti described as ahaitukī, without ulterior motive, and apratihatā, unobstructed.

Nārada's account in Canto 1, Chapters 5–6 presents devotion through biography, listening, practice, and transformation. Bhīṣma's final attention toward Krishna in Chapter 9 provides another narrative context.

D

Dharma (धर्म)

Basic sense: A term with a wide semantic range that can include order, duty, appropriate conduct, ethical responsibility, sustaining principle, and religious practice. Its meaning must be determined from context.

Bhagavata Purana: The term appears in several different settings in the series. Canto 1 presents Dharma symbolically as a bull, discusses the highest dharma, and places characters in situations involving competing obligations and appropriate conduct.

G

Guṇa (गुण)

Basic sense: quality, characteristic, attribute, or strand. In discussions of Prakṛti, the term refers to sattva, rajas, and tamas.

Bhagavata Purana: The three guṇas appear repeatedly in discussions of mind, manifestation, devotion, and Prakṛti.

Cross-text caution: The vocabulary of the three guṇas is shared across several Indic traditions, but their role should be interpreted within the framework of the particular text under discussion.

Kāla (काल)

Basic sense: time.

Bhagavata Purana: Time appears both as a subject of philosophical inquiry and as a force within narrative experience. Parīkṣit asks about time while facing his own limited remaining lifespan. Bhīṣma later reflects on Time in relation to suffering and the circumstances of the Pāṇḍavas.

Māyā (माया)

Basic sense: A multivalent Sanskrit term whose meaning varies substantially across textual and philosophical contexts.

Bhagavata Purana: Māyā appears among Parīkṣit's questions concerning God, self, body, creation, and the structure of reality.

Mukti (मुक्ति)

Basic sense: release or liberation.

Bhagavata Purana: Canto 2, Chapter 10 includes mukti among the ten characteristics of the Bhagavata and describes it in relation to relinquishing a false condition or identity and becoming established in one's own nature.

Liberation also appears within Bhīṣma's compressed account of dharma and the aims of human life in Canto 1, Chapter 9.

Bhagavata Purana: The expression appears in Canto 1, Chapter 10 in a passage concerning manifestation and the differentiated world.

Nivṛtti (निवृत्ति)

Basic sense: turning back, withdrawal, cessation, or abstention.

Bhagavata Purana: Nivṛtti appears together with pravṛtti in Bhīṣma's compressed teaching on dharma in Canto 1, Chapter 9.

Cross-text caution: The terms pravṛtti and nivṛtti occur in multiple Indic textual contexts. Their function in a particular Bhagavata passage should not automatically be identified with every later technical use.

P

Prakṛti (प्रकृति)

Basic sense: nature, material nature, constitution, or underlying material condition, depending on context.

Bhagavata Purana: Prakṛti appears in discussions of creation and manifestation, including Brahmā's role as a secondary creative agent.

Cross-text caution: Prakṛti has a highly technical role in classical Sāṃkhya. Related vocabulary in the Bhagavata should therefore be compared carefully without assuming complete doctrinal identity.

Puruṣa (पुरुष)

Basic sense: person, being, or personhood; the term also acquires specialized meanings in different philosophical traditions.

Bhagavata Purana: Canto 1, Chapter 10 describes Krishna as Puruṣaḥ Purātanaḥ, the primordial or ancient Puruṣa.

Cross-text caution: This usage should not automatically be identified with the technical plurality of puruṣas in classical Sāṃkhya. The same word participates in different conceptual frameworks.

Sattva (सत्त्व)

Basic sense: one of the three guṇas. Depending on context, it is associated with clarity, lucidity, balance, light, or intelligibility.

Bhagavata Purana: Sattva appears in discussions of mental condition, sustained hearing, devotion, knowledge, and manifestation.

Cross-text caution: Although sattva is shared vocabulary across several Indic traditions, its precise function should be established from the textual context rather than treated as a single universal psychological category.

Tapas / Tapasya (तपस् / तपस्या)

Basic sense: austerity, disciplined effort, or sustained practice.

Bhagavata Purana: Brahmā responds to uncertainty through tapas rather than claiming knowledge he does not possess. His practice precedes further knowledge and revelation.

V

Vairāgya (वैराग्य)

Basic sense: non-attachment, dispassion, or freedom from attachment.

Bhagavata Purana: Vairāgya appears in discussions of changed orientation toward possessions, status, desire, and worldly attachment. It also appears in Bhīṣma's teaching on dharma.

Cross-text caution: Vairāgya is also an important technical term in the Yoga Sutra. Its meaning and function there should be examined within the Yoga system rather than automatically transferred to every Bhagavata passage.

Visarga (विसर्ग)

Basic sense: secondary creation, differentiation, or further bringing forth, depending on context.

Bhagavata Purana: Visarga is the second of the ten characteristics described in Canto 2, Chapter 10. The episode distinguishes it from sarga, primary creation.

Vitṛṣṇā (वितृष्णा)

Basic sense: absence of thirst or craving; freedom from desire or longing.

Bhagavata Purana: The term appears in the description of Bhīṣma's final condition in Canto 1, Chapter 9.

Terminological caution: Vitṛṣṇā and vairāgya belong to a related semantic field, but they should not simply be treated as interchangeable technical terms. In Bhīṣma's final state, freedom from craving coexists with active memory, attention, praise, and relationship.

Viveka (विवेक)

Basic sense: discernment, discrimination, or the capacity to distinguish.

In this project: Viveka has been used as an interpretive term in discussions of discernment, judgment, and the ordering of knowledge in Episodes 1, 2, and 7.

Terminological caution: Where viveka is used as an interpretive category in this project, that should be distinguished from claims that a particular Bhagavata passage itself uses the Sanskrit term. Viveka also acquires specific technical significance in Yoga and other philosophical traditions.
