Sanskrit Text & Grammar

Selected grammatical notes on Sanskrit verses discussed in the Sattva Intelligence Bhagavata Purana series. Entries provide padaccheda, morphology, syntax, and literal sense where close attention to the Sanskrit contributes to interpretation.

This is a selective study resource rather than a comprehensive grammatical commentary on the Bhagavata Purana. Verses are included when their Sanskrit wording receives substantive attention in the series or is particularly useful for understanding the passage.

How to Use These Notes

Each entry begins with the Sanskrit text and IAST transliteration, followed by a grammatical separation of the words. The morphology table identifies important forms and their grammatical functions. Short notes then explain compounds, syntax, and places where the grammar affects interpretation.

The literal renderings are intentionally close to the Sanskrit. They are not intended to replace published translations. Where a grammatical construction admits interpretive difficulty, the difficulty is identified rather than concealed by a smooth English rendering.

These notes should also be read alongside the Sanskrit Terms guide. The two resources serve different purposes: the Sanskrit Terms guide follows the contextual meanings of significant vocabulary, while this page examines how particular Sanskrit forms function within particular verses.

Bhagavata Purana 1.10.21

Sanskrit

स वै किलायं पुरुषः पुरातनो
य एक आसीदविशेष आत्मनि ।
अग्रे गुणेभ्यो जगदात्मनीश्वरे
निमीलितात्मन्निशि सुप्तशक्तिषु ॥ २१ ॥

IAST

sa vai kilāyaṁ puruṣaḥ purātano
ya eka āsīd aviśeṣa ātmani |
agre guṇebhyo jagad-ātmanīśvare
nimīlitātman niśi supta-śaktiṣu || 21 ||

Padaccheda

saḥ | vai | kila | ayam | puruṣaḥ | purātanaḥ | yaḥ | ekaḥ | āsīt | aviśeṣa | ātmani | agre | guṇebhyaḥ | jagat-ātmani | īśvare | nimīlita-ātman | niśi | supta-śaktiṣu

guṇebhyaḥ guṇa, ablative plural masculine before / prior to the guṇas
jagad-ātmani Compound, locative singular in the self of the world
īśvare īśvara, locative singular masculine in the Lord
niśi niś, locative singular feminine in the night
supta-śaktiṣu Compound, locative plural when / with the powers dormant

Syntax

The principal identification is saḥ ... ayam puruṣaḥ purātanaḥ: “this is indeed that primordial Puruṣa.” The relative clause yaḥ ekaḥ āsīt, “who alone existed,” further characterizes that Puruṣa.

The latter half of the verse is grammatically compressed. The locatives and compounds describe the condition associated with cosmic withdrawal and the dormancy of powers. The expression nimīlitātman is especially difficult and should not be reduced to a simple dictionary gloss without considering the traditional syntactic interpretation.

Working Literal Rendering

This indeed is that primordial Puruṣa, who alone existed prior to the differentiated operation of the guṇas, when the powers were dormant in the night of dissolution.

This rendering is deliberately restrained. The latter half of the verse contains compressed syntax that requires commentarial clarification.

Why the Sanskrit Matters

The verse does not merely use the word puruṣa. It identifies Krishna as ayam puruṣaḥ purātanaḥ, “this primordial Puruṣa,” and characterizes him through yaḥ ekaḥ āsīt, “who alone existed.” The surrounding grammatical predicates are therefore relevant when comparing this passage with technical uses of puruṣa in other philosophical traditions.

The occurrence of the term should not by itself be treated as evidence that the verse simply reproduces the classical Sāṃkhya conception of multiple puruṣas. The Bhagavata passage must first be interpreted within its own grammatical and theological context.

Bhagavata Purana 1.10.22

Sanskrit

स एव भूयो निजवीर्यचोदितां
स्वजीवमायां प्रकृतिं सिसृक्षतीम् ।
अनामरूपात्मनि रूपनामनी
विधित्समानोऽनुससार शास्त्रकृत् ॥ २२ ॥

IAST

sa eva bhūyo nija-vīrya-coditāṁ
sva-jīva-māyāṁ prakṛtiṁ sisṛkṣatīm |
anāma-rūpātmani rūpa-nāmanī
vidhitsamāno ’nusasāra śāstra-kṛt || 22 ||

Padaccheda

saḥ | eva | bhūyaḥ | nija-vīrya-coditām | sva-jīva-māyām | prakṛtim | sisṛkṣatīm | anāma-rūpa-ātmani | rūpa-nāmanī | vidhitsamānaḥ | anusasāra | śāstra-kṛt

Selected Morphology

Form Analysis Basic sense here
saḥ Pronoun, nominative singular masculine he; that one
eva Indeclinable indeed; that very
bhūyaḥ Indeclinable again
nija-vīrya-coditām Accusative singular feminine, agreeing with prakṛtim impelled by his own power
sva-jīva-māyām Accusative singular feminine compound māyā associated with his jīvas
prakṛtim prakṛti, accusative singular feminine prakṛti; nature
sisṛkṣatīm Desiderative participle, accusative singular feminine desiring / preparing to create
anāma-rūpātmani Compound, locative singular in the self without material name and form
rūpa-nāmanī Accusative dual neuter form and name
vidhitsamānaḥ Desiderative participle, nominative singular masculine intending to establish or assign
anusasāra Verbal form from anu + √sṛ followed; resorted to; entered into relation with
śāstra-kṛt Compound, nominative singular masculine maker / producer of scripture

Grammatical Relationships

The central accusative noun is prakṛtim. Several feminine accusative singular forms belong with it: nija-vīrya-coditām, sva-jīva-māyām, and sisṛkṣatīm. The morphology therefore shows that these expressions form a grammatical cluster describing prakṛti rather than a series of unrelated concepts.

The form sisṛkṣatīm is desiderative and describes prakṛti as intending or preparing to create. By contrast, vidhitsamānaḥ is nominative singular masculine and refers back to the subject, saḥ. The verse therefore distinguishes grammatically between the activity attributed to prakṛti and the intention attributed to the Puruṣa.

The form rūpa-nāmanī is accusative dual: “form and name.” The dual number makes the pairing grammatically explicit.

Working Literal Rendering

That very one again, intending to assign form and name to the self without material name and form, followed prakṛti, impelled by his own power and intent on creation, he who is the maker of scripture.

This is a working grammatical rendering rather than an attempt to replace published translations. Compounds such as sva-jīva-māyām and anāma-rūpātmani involve interpretive questions that require attention to commentary and context.

Why the Sanskrit Matters

Verse 1.10.22 begins with sa eva, “that very same one,” linking its subject directly with the Puruṣa described in the preceding verse. It then introduces prakṛtim as grammatically distinct from that subject.

Read together, 1.10.21 and 1.10.22 therefore provide a textual basis for examining how the Bhagavata itself relates Puruṣa, prakṛti, guṇas, jīva, name and form, and creation. These relationships should be established from the passage before importing the complete technical structure of another philosophical system.
