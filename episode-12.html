Vidura’s Journey and Krishna’s Final Days

Vidura’s Departure, Uddhava’s Remembrance, and the End of the Yādavas

Canto 3, Chapters 1–4

Episode Overview

Why did Vidura leave the Kuru court? What happened to the powerful Yādava household? And how does Uddhava remember Kṛṣṇa’s final days? Episode 12 follows the opening four chapters of the Third Canto, beginning with Vidura’s departure from Hastināpura and continuing through his meeting with Uddhava beside the Yamunā.

Uddhava’s recollections move between Kṛṣṇa’s childhood in Vraja, his royal activities, the aftermath of Kurukṣetra, and the destruction of the Yādavas. The episode closes with Uddhava’s final encounter with Kṛṣṇa and Vidura’s movement toward Maitreya.

Text Covered

Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 3, Chapters 1–4. Chapter 1 recounts Vidura’s departure and pilgrimage. Chapter 2 presents Uddhava’s grief and memories of Kṛṣṇa. Chapter 3 surveys Kṛṣṇa’s activities and the events leading toward the Yādavas’ destruction. Chapter 4 describes Uddhava’s final meeting with Kṛṣṇa and his direction of Vidura toward Maitreya.

The principal English source is G. V. Tagare’s translation of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 3, Chapters 1–4 (Motilal Banarsidass). Key Textual and Philosophical Notes

1. Vidura’s Counsel and Departure (3.1)

Vidura advises Dhṛtarāṣṭra to recognize the injustice done to the Pāṇḍavas and to restrain Duryodhana. Duryodhana’s insult precipitates Vidura’s departure. His response raises a question about the relationship between moral judgment, personal dignity, and restraint when counsel is rejected.

2. Uddhava’s Remembrance (3.2)

Uddhava’s grief leads to recollections of Kṛṣṇa’s manifestation and activities. He remembers the grace extended to Pūtanā, Kṛṣṇa’s life among the cowherds, the restoration of the Yamunā after Kāliya’s presence, the shelter provided beneath Govardhana, and the autumnal rāsa. These events are recalled briefly in this chapter; the full narratives belong elsewhere in the Bhāgavata.

3. The Remaining Burden of the Earth (3.3.12–15)

Following the destruction of armies at Kurukṣetra, Kṛṣṇa reflects that the earth’s burden remains because of the Yādavas’ military strength. The passage anticipates internal conflict among the Yādavas. This is the immediate textual setting for Kṛṣṇa’s dissatisfaction after the war, rather than an explicit statement that he is grieving for Duryodhana.

4. The Final Meeting and Maitreya (3.4)

Uddhava recalls encountering Kṛṣṇa near the Sarasvatī, with Maitreya present. The chapter refers to Kṛṣṇa’s instruction to Uddhava without reproducing the full teaching. Uddhava subsequently directs Vidura toward Maitreya, preparing the dialogue that begins in Chapter 5.

Key Sanskrit Terms

Kāla (काल; kā-la) — time, especially in relation to change and dissolution.

Māyā (माया; mā-yā) — power of manifestation; its precise meaning depends on the passage.

Akṣauhiṇī (अक्षौहिणी; ak-shau-hi-ṇī) — a large military formation in Sanskrit epic literature.

Rāsa (रास; rā-sa) — the dance recalled briefly in 3.2.34.

Discussion Question

Vidura departs after his counsel is rejected, while Uddhava remembers a divine figure whose power did not prevent the destruction of his own household. How does the Bhāgavata bring together moral responsibility, the limits of political power, and the continuation of spiritual instruction?

Connections Across the Series

Episode 11 narrated Vidura’s return to the Kuru household in the First Canto. Episode 12 returns to the earlier circumstances of his departure, now narrated in the Third Canto. This change of narrative perspective also prepares the transition to Vidura’s dialogue with Maitreya.
