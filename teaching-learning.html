Academic teaching · 15 November 2026

Selected Teaching Engagements

Human Discernment in the Age of AI: Sāṃkhya, Yoga, and the Bhagavad Gītā

Oxford Centre for Hindu Studies (OCHS)

Oxford, United Kingdom

Online teaching demonstration · 15 November 2026

This teaching demonstration for the Oxford Centre for Hindu Studies continuing education programme explored a question relevant to education, professional life, and everyday decision-making: when we delegate cognitive work to artificial intelligence, what happens to our own judgment?

The session opened with a 2023 New York legal case in which a lawyer submitted fictitious judicial decisions generated by ChatGPT. The case invited discussion of the difference between receiving information, evaluating its reliability, and taking responsibility for decisions made on its basis.

Close reading then focused on three classical Indian philosophical texts:

  • Sāṃkhya Kārikā 20 and 23: The relationship between buddhi (cognitive determination), prakṛti, and puruṣa (consciousness). These distinctions clarify the Sāṃkhya account of cognition without treating its metaphysical categories as descriptions of AI.
  • Patañjali's Yoga Sūtra 2.5 and 2.26: Avidyā as mistaken apprehension, rather than merely a lack of information, and viveka-khyāti as discriminative knowledge within Yoga's philosophical framework.
  • Bhagavad Gītā 2.7, 18.63, and 18.73: Arjuna's uncertainty, Kṛṣṇa's instruction to deliberate, and the relationship between receiving guidance, understanding, and acting.

These works do not anticipate artificial intelligence. Instead, their carefully contextualized philosophical distinctions can help frame questions about knowledge, discernment, cognitive delegation, and human responsibility. The demonstration combined selected Sanskrit passages, contemporary examples, and participant discussion.

Teaching resources and independent study

How to use these materials

Sattva Intelligence is an independent, open-access companion to the study of classical Indic texts and philosophical traditions. Instructors may select individual episodes, passages, and explanatory resources to supplement assigned primary readings. Students can move between the narrative context, Sanskrit forms, and thematic indexes. These pathways are suggestions, not complete courses or accredited instructional modules.

A practical classroom approach

Choose a primary passage from the assigned text, use an episode or study note to establish context, compare the original wording with a scholarly translation, and discuss which conclusions are grounded in the passage and which are interpretive. Students should cite the primary text and relevant scholarly editions or translations, not rely on this website alone.
