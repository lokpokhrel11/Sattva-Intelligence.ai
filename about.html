About

Lok Pokhrel

Lok Pokhrel is a Visiting Research Associate in the Department of Religion at Rutgers University and a communication scholar based in the Vancouver area. His work sits at the intersection of classical Indic thought, communication ethics, technology studies, and comparative philosophy.

His current research includes sustained engagement with the Bhagavad Gita, Yoga Sutras, Bhagavata Purana, Samkhya, and Nyaya traditions, with particular attention to questions of human judgment, responsibility, attention, and discernment. His work includes engagement with Sanskrit terminology and close use of translations and commentarial sources.

In preparing the Bhagavata Purana project, he works comparatively across primary translations and scholarly commentary, with attention to textual context, differences among translations, and the distinction between what a text states and what may reasonably be interpreted from it.

His current research asks what classical Indic traditions can contribute to contemporary thinking about attention, mind, restraint, discernment, judgment, and responsibility under conditions of technological acceleration. These questions also inform his developing book project, Sattva Intelligence: A Dharmic Ethics Framework for Human Alignment and Responsible AI Delegation. The project approaches AI alignment primarily as a problem of human ethical judgment and responsibility, particularly when cognitive activities and decisions are increasingly delegated to technological systems.

His research has received the International Communication Association's Best Paper Award and the Association for Education in Journalism and Mass Communication's Carol Burnett Award. He previously served as a Strategic Communications Fellow at the United Nations Division for Ocean Affairs and Law of the Sea. He has taught communication at Washington State University, Southeast Missouri State University, Minnesota State University, and Western Washington University, and previously served as Subject Lead in Communications at University Canada West.

About the Project

The work documented on this site begins with close engagement with classical texts rather than with an attempt to find ancient equivalents for contemporary concepts. Sources are named, interpretive claims are distinguished from translations, and substantive errors are corrected publicly. Comparisons with contemporary philosophy, psychology, communication, and technology are introduced where they clarify a genuine problem raised by the text.

The main series reads the Bhagavata Purana chapter by chapter, allowing its narrative, philosophical, and ethical questions to develop within their textual setting. Indic Minds examines the Nyaya Sutra and related traditions of reasoning, knowledge, inference, and argument. Indic Wisdom develops selected conceptual questions that emerge from engagement with classical Indic thought and places them in careful conversation with contemporary problems.

Across these strands, a larger research question is gradually taking shape: what capacities make responsible human judgment possible when increasingly powerful cognitive activities can be delegated to technological systems? Questions of attention, mind, avidya, guna, restraint, discernment, judgment, responsibility, and delegation provide different points of entry into that inquiry.

The project's research practices and source standards are described on the Method and Sources page. Substantive corrections are maintained in the Corrections Log.
