About

Lok Pokhrel

Lok Pokhrel is a Visiting Research Associate in the Department of Religion at Rutgers University and a communication scholar based in the Vancouver area. His work sits at the intersection of classical Indic thought, communication ethics, technology studies, and comparative philosophy.

His current research includes sustained engagement with the Bhagavad Gita, Yoga Sutras, Bhagavata Purana, Samkhya, and Nyaya traditions, with particular attention to questions of human judgment, responsibility, attention, and discernment. His work includes engagement with Sanskrit terminology and close use of translations and commentarial sources.

In preparing the Bhagavata Purana project, he works comparatively across primary translations and scholarly commentary, with attention to textual context, differences among translations, and the distinction between what a text states and what may reasonably be interpreted from it.

His current research asks what classical Indic traditions can contribute to contemporary thinking about attention, mind, restraint, discernment, judgment, and responsibility under conditions of technological acceleration. These questions also inform his developing book project, Sattva Intelligence: A Dharmic Ethics Framework for Human Alignment and Responsible AI Delegation. The project approaches AI alignment primarily as a problem of human ethical judgment and responsibility, particularly when cognitive activities and decisions are increasingly delegated to technological systems.

His research has received the International Communication Association's Best Paper Award and the Association for Education in Journalism and Mass Communication's Carol Burnett Award. He previously served as a Strategic Communications Fellow at the United Nations Division for Ocean Affairs and Law of the Sea.
