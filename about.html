About

Lok Pokhrel

Communication Ethics · Classical Indic Philosophy · Human Judgment and AI

Academic Profile

Lok Pokhrel is a Visiting Research Associate in the Department of Religion at Rutgers University and a communication scholar based in the Vancouver area of Canada. His research brings communication theory and ethics into conversation with classical Indic philosophy, with particular attention to human cognition, judgment, and responsibility.

His academic background is in communication and media studies. His professional experience also includes strategic communications in academic, nonprofit, and international settings, including a fellowship with the United Nations Division for Ocean Affairs and Law of the Sea.

His scholarly work has received recognition from the International Communication Association and the Association for Education in Journalism and Mass Communication. He has taught communication at universities in Canada and the United States.

Research Orientation

My research examines how changes in communication and technology affect human attention, judgment, and ethical responsibility. I approach these questions through communication theory and classical Indic philosophy, two areas of inquiry that have increasingly come together in my work.

A recurring concern is the difference between having the ability to make a judgment and being able to exercise that ability under actual social, cognitive, and technological conditions. This question arises in mediated communication, in the study of classical philosophical traditions, and increasingly in human interactions with artificial intelligence.

Principal Research Areas

Communication Theory and Media Ethics

My work in communication studies examines how mediated environments shape attention, recognition, evaluation, and ethical judgment. I am particularly interested in the conditions under which people can respond thoughtfully to information rather than being directed primarily by speed, emotional pressure, or established patterns of engagement.

Two current lines of inquiry develop these concerns. The Advaita and Spanda Communication Model (ASCM) examines recognition and meaningful exchange in digital communication. I am exploring its application to public discourse on artificial intelligence and higher education.

A related project, Sattva-Based Cognitive Alignment Theory, examines the conditions under which a person's capacity for evaluation and judgment is actually exercised in mediated environments. This work connects communication theory with questions of attention, cognitive states, and ethical agency.

Classical Indic Philosophy and Textual Studies

My engagement with classical Indic philosophy draws on the Bhagavad Gītā, Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Nyāya, and non-dual philosophical traditions associated with Kashmir Śaivism. My familiarity with the Bhāgavata Purāṇa began in childhood through family and community, while my closer scholarly engagement with other classical sources developed alongside my academic research.

I begin with the textual and philosophical settings of these traditions. Their concepts are not treated as ancient versions of modern scientific or technological ideas. Where they offer resources for examining contemporary questions, I distinguish their meanings within the classical traditions from the more limited ways in which I apply them today.

This work includes close attention to Sanskrit terminology, textual context, translations, commentarial traditions, and, for selected passages, grammatical features relevant to interpretation.

Human Judgment and Responsible AI Delegation

A major current direction of my research concerns what happens to human judgment when increasingly complex cognitive activities are delegated to artificial intelligence. AI systems can summarize, compare, draft, recommend, and assist in evaluation. Their ability to perform these tasks, however, does not by itself settle what should be delegated or when an earlier decision to delegate should be reconsidered.

I am developing the concept of Delegated Momentum to examine how authorized machine-mediated activity may continue under an earlier instruction even when renewed human judgment has become necessary. The question is not only what people should delegate to AI, but how they remain capable of governing what they have already delegated.

My developing book, Sattva Intelligence: A Dharmic Ethics Framework for Human Alignment and Responsible AI Delegation, examines these questions through communication ethics, comparative philosophy, and classical Indic accounts of cognition, discernment, and restraint.

Hindu Studies and Nepal

My research also examines philosophical and ethical questions within lived Hindu traditions, independently of their possible applications to artificial intelligence.

A paper submitted for journal review, Reverence and Asymmetry: Dhog, Gender, and Relational Authority in Hindu Nepal, studies the Nepali practice of dhog through Dharma literature, Purāṇic sources, and ethnographic scholarship. It considers questions of reverence, gender, kinship, and relational authority.

About the Sattva Intelligence Website

This website brings together research, textual interpretation, and public-facing educational work. Its principal focus is the close study of classical Indic texts and the philosophical questions that emerge from them.

Bhagavata Purana

The Bhagavata Purana series follows the text chapter by chapter, attending to its narrative, philosophical, and ethical development. Companion pages provide references, conceptual explanations, and selected Sanskrit textual and grammatical notes.

Indic Minds

Indic Minds explores the Nyāya tradition, including questions of knowledge, reasoning, inference, evidence, and argument. It also considers the relevance of these questions to contemporary intellectual and communicative life.

Indic Wisdom

Indic Wisdom presents selected essays and recorded discussions that bring classical Indic thought into conversation with questions arising in contemporary society, culture, communication, and ethics.

Academic and Educational Use

Explore suggested study pathways on the Teaching & Learning page.

Sattva Intelligence is an independent, open-access educational and research resource for the study of classical Indic texts and philosophical traditions. Its selected primary passages are accompanied by Sanskrit terminology, grammatical observations, conceptual explanations, and interpretive discussions.

University instructors may use relevant materials as supplementary resources in courses on Hindu Studies, Religious Studies, Sanskrit, South Asian Studies, comparative philosophy, and related subjects. Students can use the episode discussions, Sanskrit Text & Grammar, Sanskrit Terms, Verse Index, and Concept Index to support assigned reading and independent study.

These resources complement rather than replace critical editions, established translations, and specialist scholarship. Readers are encouraged to consult the primary sources and references identified on the site and its Method and Sources page. Instructors interested in using the materials or discussing their classroom application are welcome to contact the author.
