After Doubt Is Resolved: Vidura, Maitreya, and Continuing Inquiry

A close comparative reading of Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.7.15–20, in conversation with its Sanskrit commentaries.

Return to Episode 13

Research status: Scholarly working study, not peer reviewed. Sanskrit readings below are transcribed from a digital Canto 3 multi-commentary compilation; contested readings have not yet been independently checked against printed editions.

The interpretive problem

At Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.7.15, Vidura announces that Maitreya's words have removed his doubt. Yet in 3.7.18 he still speaks of dispelling something through service at Maitreya's feet. The question is not whether knowledge or devotion is superior in general. It is what remains unresolved in the specific sequence, and how the commentators construe that remainder.

Bhāgavata 3.7.18: The object of removal

अर्थाभावं विनिश्चित्य प्रतीतस्यापि नात्मनः ।
तां चापि युष्मच्चरणसेवयाहं पराणुदे ॥

Working interpretive rendering: Having ascertained the absence of reality concerning what nevertheless appears as non-self, I shall also dispel that [remaining condition] through service at your feet. The bracketed phrase deliberately leaves open the contested referent of tām; the commentators construe the verse differently.

Śrīdhara Svāmin: A persisting appearance

kevalaṁ bādhitānuvṛttir evāvaśiṣṭā

Śrīdhara reads the relevant non-self as anātmanaḥ prapañcasya, the phenomenal world understood as non-self. His gloss tāṁ pratītim apy ahaṁ parāṇude identifies what is to be dispelled as pratīti, the appearance that persists after its independent reality has been negated. The technical expression bādhitānuvṛtti is present in the supplied digital witness. This does not mean that prior understanding has accomplished nothing.

Viśvanātha Cakravartin: A provisional contrasting reading

The supplied electronic compilation reads tām apratītim ahaṁ parāṇude, whereas Śrīdhara reads tāṁ pratītim apy ahaṁ parāṇude. If the transcription is accurate, this contrast changes the immediate object of removal from an appearance to a failure of apprehension. This is a provisional textual observation, not an independently collated variant or an established doctrinal disagreement. A separately printed Canto 3 witness was identified but its relevant page could not be accessed for verification.

Bhāgavata 3.7.19: More than the removal of error

Jīva Gosvāmin asks prapañca-pratīti-parāṇodana-mātraṁ kiyat?, questioning the adequacy of merely removing phenomenal appearance as the measure of attainment. His description of the removal of distress as ānuṣaṅgika, incidental or accompanying, shifts attention toward positive devotional fulfillment. This is a specific commentarial claim, not an assertion that discernment is irrelevant.

Bhāgavata 3.7.20: Three distinct questions about association

Śrīdhara's explanation moves from service to great devotees (mahat-sevā) through hearing Hari's narrative (hari-kathā-śravaṇa) toward love (prema). Viśvanātha emphasizes that such service is attainable by devotees' grace (tat-kṛpaika-labhyatvena), not as a guaranteed product of austerity.

A longer interpretive passage attributed to Sanātana Gosvāmin in the working research materials proposes alternative ways of understanding saintly association. Because the exact wording and attribution have not been confirmed in an independent Digdarśinī witness, this page does not reproduce or rely on that passage as authenticated primary evidence.

These readings allow three separate questions: (1) what cognitive condition remains, (2) whether association is a means, an end, or both, and (3) how association becomes available. The third question substantially overlaps with existing scholarship.

Relation to previous research

Jonathan Edelmann's 2015 study examines the cause of devotion, spontaneous encounters with devotees, and the relationship between sādhu-saṅga, faith, and bhakti. It also discusses Śrīdhara and Viśvanātha in connection with Bhāgavata 11.20.8. Therefore neither the importance of saintly association nor the existence of commentarial disagreement can be claimed as new here. The narrower contribution proposed on this page is the comparison of residual apprehension after Vidura's declared resolution of doubt in 3.7.15–20.

Source provenance and independent witnesses

The digital five-commentary compilation has an identifiable editorial lineage. The Jiva Gaudiya Grantha Mandira catalogue identifies its Canto 3 sources as Śrīdhara's Bhāvārthadīpikā (Ramateja Pandeya edition, Chowkhamba, 1996), Jīva's Krama-sandarbha (Puridasa Mahasaya, 1952), and Viśvanātha's Sārārtha-darśinī (Bhakti Ballabh Tirtha edition, 1995). The catalogue also identifies the compilation as an edited electronic transcription rather than a facsimile of any one printed edition. This establishes provenance, not the correctness of every transmitted reading.

For a genuinely independent comparison, the Vedic Preservation Initiative's bibliography points to an 1860 printed Śrīdhara commentary and a 1965 printed Sārārtha-darśinī (Book 3). These are candidate witnesses for collation; their readings at 3.7.18 have not yet been checked in this study. The IGNCA electronic Hari-bhakti-vilāsa is another potentially useful source, but it is not yet established here that it contains the precise Digdarśinī gloss quoted above.

Verification and editorial status

The research has completed its electronic-text review and source-provenance audit, but not an independent printed-edition collation. The 1965 printed Sārārtha-darśinī Book 3 is catalogued online; its hosted download could not be retrieved during this review. The Sanātana-attributed gloss likewise remains unverified. Neither reading is treated here as established critical text. The Edelmann comparison is bibliographic and thematic, not a page-by-page collation. This page is not a critical edition, peer-reviewed publication, or claim of established originality.
