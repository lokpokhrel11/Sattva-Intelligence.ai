After Doubt Is Resolved: Vidura, Maitreya, and Continuing Inquiry

A close comparative reading of Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.7.15–20, in conversation with its Sanskrit commentaries.

Return to Episode 13

Research question

Why does Vidura continue to seek transformation through service after declaring that his doubt has been resolved? The sequence from 3.7.15 to 3.7.20 raises a distinction between resolving a metaphysical question and the further work of apprehension, practice, and devotional association.

Reading the sequence

3.7.15: Doubt resolved

Vidura acknowledges that Maitreya's instruction has cut through his doubt. This declaration should not be treated as the end of the dialogue's intellectual and spiritual work.

3.7.17–18: The remaining problem

The commentators differ in how they construe what remains. Śrīdhara Svāmin identifies a persisting appearance (pratīti) and invokes bādhitānuvṛtti, the continuation of appearance following its negation. Viśvanātha Cakravartin construes the problem in terms of incomplete apprehension (apratīti). The grammatical difference is significant; it does not by itself prove doctrinal opposition.

3.7.19–20: Service and association

The passage moves toward service and the company of devotees. Śrīdhara emphasizes a movement through association and hearing toward love of Hari. Later Gauḍīya discussions allow association to be understood not only instrumentally but also as intrinsically spiritually valuable. Attribution and textual context of the extended glosses require independent edition-level checking.

Scope and method

This page presents a focused, provisional comparative interpretation, not a peer-reviewed article or a claim to have discovered the significance of devotional association. Readings are drawn from a digital multi-commentary compilation; disputed readings have not all been collated against printed editions.

Relevant scholarship

Jonathan Edelmann, “The Cause of Devotion in Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Theology,” Journal of the American Oriental Society 135, no. 1 (2015): 49–69. DOI. Edelmann's analysis of encounters with devotees is an important antecedent; the present study asks a narrower question about Vidura's transition from resolved doubt to continuing practice.

Gopal K. Gupta, Māyā in the Bhāgavata Purāṇa: Human Suffering and Divine Play (Oxford University Press, 2020). DOI.

Verification status

Independent printed-edition verification of the contested Viśvanātha reading and the longer gloss attributed to Sanātana remains outstanding. These limitations should be resolved before advancing stronger claims about commentator disagreement or originality.
