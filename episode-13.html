Why Do We Suffer? Vidura’s Questions on Creation and Consciousness

Vidura and Maitreya on manifestation, māyā, consciousness, and bondage

Canto 3, Chapters 5–7 · Video published October 27, 2026

Watch Episode 13 Return to Episode Index

Episode Overview

Vidura comes to Maitreya asking why the human pursuit of happiness can lead to further suffering. The discussion moves through the origins of the manifested world, the emergence and coordination of its principles, the universal person, and the problem of bondage. The episode closes with Maitreya’s account of devotion and Vidura’s praise of spiritual fearlessness.

Text Covered

Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 3, Chapters 5–7. Chapter 5 introduces Vidura’s inquiry and the principles of creation. Chapter 6 presents the Virāṭ Puruṣa, the faculties and their presiding powers, cosmic and social order, and the limits of description. Chapter 7 addresses divine activity, māyā, apparent bondage, devotion, and the gift of fearlessness.

Primary Source and Method

The primary text is the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 3, Chapters 5–7. G. V. Tagare’s English translation (Motilal Banarsidass) is a principal reference. The episode distinguishes Sanskrit textual claims from interpretive comparisons and does not equate the Bhāgavata’s devotional cosmology with every other Sāṃkhya or Vedānta account.

Key Textual and Philosophical Notes

1. Vidura’s Question and the Beginning of Creation (3.5)

Vidura asks why actions undertaken for happiness so often fail to relieve suffering. Maitreya’s answer turns toward Bhagavān before manifestation, the emergence of māyā, kāla, mahat and ahaṃkāra, and the differentiation of the guṇas, faculties and elements.

2. Why the Deities Cannot Act Separately (3.5.38–51)

The presiding deities possess distinct capacities yet cannot accomplish their work independently. Their prayer places the order of creation within dependence upon Bhagavān and reconnects cosmology to suffering, refuge and devotion.

3. The Virāṭ Puruṣa and Threefold Experience (3.6)

The Lord’s entry activates the principles of manifestation. The universal person provides a framework for the senses, their presiding deities and their objects. The distinctions adhyātma, adhidaiva and adhibhūta are especially important.

4. The Cosmic Body, Society and the Limits of Description (3.6)

The chapter relates cosmic regions and four varṇas to the universal body. Its religious ordering and the precedence assigned to the brāhmaṇa require careful historical interpretation. Maitreya also acknowledges the limits of speech and mind in describing Bhagavān.

5. Divine Action, Māyā and Bondage (3.7.2–11)

Vidura asks how qualities and actions, even as līlā, can belong to the unchanging, nirguṇa Lord. He presses the related problem of how a living being can experience bondage and suffering. Maitreya responds with analogies of dream and reflection.

6. Devotion, Resolution of Doubts and Fearlessness (3.7.12–41)

Maitreya describes a gradual loosening of misconception through devotional discipline, grace and hearing of the Lord’s qualities. Vidura acknowledges the resolution of his doubts, continues asking about the world and religious duties, and closes by praising the gift of fearlessness to living beings.

Key Sanskrit Terms

Māyā (माया; mā-yā) — The Lord’s power of manifestation and, in Chapter 7, the setting of apparent bondage.

Kāla (काल; kā-la) — Time as an ordering factor in manifestation.

Mahat (महत्; ma-hat) — The great principle in the cosmological sequence.

Ahaṃkāra (अहंकार; a-haṃ-kā-ra) — The differentiating ‘I’-principle, not simply personal arrogance.

Guṇas (गुण; gu-ṇa) — Sattva, rajas and tamas, the three modes of material nature.

Virāṭ Puruṣa (विराट् पुरुष; vi-rāṭ pu-ru-ṣa) — The universal person through whose body cosmic functions are described.

Adhyātma, Adhidaiva, Adhibhūta (अध्यात्म, अधिदैव, अधिभूत) — The personal faculty, presiding divine power and object of experience.

Līlā (लीला; lī-lā) — Divine play, raised as a philosophical question rather than a sufficient explanation by itself.

Bhagavad-bhakti-yoga (भगवद्भक्तियोग) — Devotional discipline within Maitreya’s account of freedom from bondage.

Jīvābhayapradāna (जीवाभयप्रदान) — Giving fearlessness to living beings (3.7.41).

Discussion Question
