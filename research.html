Research Profile

Lok Pokhrel is a Visiting Research Associate in the Department of Religion at Rutgers University. His work brings together classical Indic thought, communication ethics, technology studies, and comparative philosophy.

His current research engages the Bhagavad Gita, Yoga Sutras, Bhagavata Purana, Samkhya, and Nyaya in relation to questions of human judgment, attention, responsibility, discernment, and technological delegation.

His academic background is in communication, media, and ethics, with teaching and research experience in universities in Canada and the United States.

Current Research

Sattva Intelligence

The central research project examines the ethical capacities required for responsible human judgment when increasingly powerful cognitive activities can be delegated to technological systems.

The project develops a framework drawing on classical Indic accounts of attention, restraint, discernment, action, and responsibility while maintaining distinctions among the philosophical and textual traditions from which these concepts emerge.

Advaita-Spanda Communication Model

The Advaita-Spanda Communication Model (ASCM) explores relational and non-dual approaches to communication, ethical interaction, and digital public discourse. The project brings classical Indian philosophical resources into conversation with contemporary communication theory without treating either tradition as reducible to the other.

Classical Indic Texts and Public Humanities

A further area of research concerns the interpretation of classical Indic texts for contemporary public audiences. This work includes the Bhagavata Purana, Nyaya, Samkhya, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Yoga Sutras, with particular attention to judgment, argument, moral psychology, responsibility, and human agency.

Book Project

Sattva Intelligence: A Dharmic Ethics Framework for Human Alignment and Responsible AI Delegation

This book project examines artificial intelligence through the problem of human delegation. Its central argument is that AI systems extend human and institutional intention across increasing levels of speed and scale, making the quality of human judgment, restraint, attention, and accountability central to questions of responsible technological use.

The project develops a Dharmic Intelligence Framework through sustained engagement with classical Indic texts and contemporary scholarship in ethics, communication, technology, and moral psychology.

Research Method

The research method distinguishes textual evidence from interpretation and comparison. Primary texts and established translations are used according to the level of the claim being made, while broader philosophical comparisons are introduced only where they preserve consequential differences among traditions.

The public-facing projects also maintain an editorial correction record so that textual or interpretive errors can be documented rather than silently removed.
