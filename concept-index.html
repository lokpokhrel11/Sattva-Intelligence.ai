Concept Index

A growing index of philosophical, ethical, and textual concepts discussed across the Bhagavata Purana series. Entries identify episodes and passages in which a concept receives substantive treatment.

Return to Episode Index

How to Use This Index

The Episode Index follows the Bhagavata Purana chapter by chapter. This Concept Index provides another way into the series. It allows readers, students, and researchers to follow philosophical and ethical questions across different episodes and textual settings.

Entries are included where a concept receives substantive discussion, rather than every time a term happens to appear. Where useful, the index also identifies cross-textual comparison with traditions such as classical Sāṃkhya, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Yoga Sutra.

For Episodes 1–7, links currently lead to the complete episode. From Episode 8 onward, precise timestamps are provided where available. Earlier entries may be given more precise timestamps in the future.

A

Āśraya (आश्रय)

Episode 5 — Canto 2, Chapter 10
Āśraya is the tenth of the Bhagavata Purana's ten characteristics. The episode considers several ways of rendering the term, including support, resort, substratum, and ultimate ground. The first nine characteristics are presented as ways of making this tenth principle intelligible. The episode therefore reads the tenfold list not simply as ten equal topics, but as nine approaches toward an underlying ground.

Aśvamedha (अश्वमेध)

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 12
Yudhishthira's postwar sacrifices, the depleted treasury, and the wealth associated with King Marutta. The passage forms part of the larger movement from war toward restored rule.

Divine Descent (Avatāra / अवतार)

Episode 4 — Canto 2, Chapter 7
The episode examines the avatāras as different forms of divine intervention. Rather than treating the chapter simply as a list, it identifies a recurring pattern: the form of intervention corresponds to the character of the problem. Varāha, Narasiṃha, Matsya, Kapila, Dhanvantari, Vyāsa, and other figures illustrate different forms of protection, teaching, healing, and the preservation of knowledge.

Brahmā: Creation and Secondary Agency

Episode 3 — Canto 2, Chapters 4–5
Brahmā is presented as a creator operating within a reality whose ultimate source does not lie in himself. The episode distinguishes his extraordinary creative agency from the question of the ultimate source of existence and examines the limits of agency within a larger order.

Episode 4 — Canto 2, Chapter 6
Brahmā places himself within the cosmic order he describes. He is a powerful agent of creation, but not the ultimate source of the materials with which he works. The episode uses this distinction to examine the difference between making, arranging, and originating.

D

Ten Characteristics of the Bhagavata Purana (Daśa-lakṣaṇa / दश-लक्षण)

Episode 5 — Canto 2, Chapter 10
The Bhagavata Purana pauses its narrative to describe its own subject matter through ten characteristics: Sarga, Visarga, Sthāna, Poṣaṇa, Ūti, Manvantara, Īśānukathā, Nirodha, Mukti, and Āśraya. The episode examines this passage as an instance of textual self-description: the Purana identifies the categories through which it understands its own knowledge, narratives, cosmology, and ultimate orientation.

Parīkṣit learns that he has seven days to live. The episode examines how the knowledge of approaching death reorganizes his priorities and leads him to ask what is worth hearing, knowing, and doing when time is limited.

Three Guṇas (Triguṇa / त्रिगुण)

Episode 2 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
An extended introduction to sattva, rajas, and tamas as three guṇas that shape perception, thought, and action. The episode asks how these different conditions influence judgment and develops the practical question: which one is running me right now?

I

Inquiry / The Discipline of Questioning

Episode 4 — Canto 2, Chapter 8
Facing death, Parīkṣit responds not by withdrawing from inquiry but by asking a wide range of questions about self and body, God and māyā, time, karma, social duty, cosmology, and conduct in emergencies. The episode presents Chapter 8 as a kind of syllabus for much of the Purana that follows, because Śuka's subsequent teaching responds to these questions.

Narrative as Knowledge (Īśānukathā / ईशानुकथा)

Episode 5 — Canto 2, Chapter 10
Īśānukathā, accounts of the Lord and those devoted to him, appears as one of the ten characteristics of the Bhagavata Purana. The episode uses its place in the list to examine narrative as a form of knowledge rather than as decoration added to philosophical analysis. Analytical categories and stories appear within the same tenfold account of what the text contains.

Judgment / Acting Before Understanding

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
Śṛṅgi hears a report about Parīkṣit's conduct, does not verify what happened, and imposes a fatal curse. His father, Śamīka, judges the punishment disproportionate. The episode uses the scene to examine rapid judgment based on incomplete information.

Episode 3 — Canto 2, Chapters 4–5
Brahmā confronts the limits of his knowledge about his own origin and points beyond himself toward Nārāyaṇa. The episode examines the difference between possessing extraordinary knowledge and knowing the ultimate source of one's own existence, while preserving the questions that remain unresolved at this stage of the narrative.

Māyā (माया)

Episode 4 — Canto 2, Chapter 8
Māyā enters the episode through one of Parīkṣit's questions: where does the Lord of māyā abide when māyā is laid aside? The concept appears within the king's larger inquiry into the relation among God, self, body, creation, and the structure of reality.

Liberation (Mukti / मुक्ति)

Episode 5 — Canto 2, Chapter 10
Mukti is the ninth characteristic in the Bhagavata Purana's tenfold self-description. In this passage it is described not primarily as going to another place, but as relinquishing a false identity and becoming established in one's own nature. The episode interprets this as liberation through subtraction rather than acquisition.

S

Creation: Sarga and Visarga (सर्ग / विसर्ग)

Episode 5 — Canto 2, Chapter 10
The first two characteristics distinguish two forms of creation. Sarga refers to primary creation: the emergence of the elements, senses, and basic materials of a universe. Visarga refers to subsequent differentiation and arrangement from materials already present. The episode connects Brahmā's creative activity particularly with Visarga and uses the distinction to separate originating the materials of creation from arranging them.

Cosmic Form / Cosmic Person (Virāṭ-rūpa / Virāṭ Puruṣa / विराट्-रूप / विराट् पुरुष)

Episode 3 — Canto 2, Chapters 1–2
The universe is contemplated as a single cosmic body. The episode examines the Virāṭ-rūpa as a concrete and comprehensive object of meditation through which attention can be gathered and directed beyond ordinary fragmentation.

Watch Episode 3

Episode 4 — Canto 2, Chapter 6
The cosmic-body teaching is developed into a larger cosmological map. Natural phenomena, the Vedas, ritual, forms of work, and human society are placed within the body of the cosmic person. The episode emphasizes that Brahmā himself remains within this larger order rather than standing outside it.

Watch Episode 4

Episode 5 — Canto 2, Chapter 10
The cosmic person appears again in the chapter devoted to the Purana's tenfold self-description. The episode identifies this as the third major use of the image in Canto 2: first as a contemplative practice, then as Brahmā's cosmological account, and finally within the text's explanation of its own method. The recurrence illustrates how the Bhagavata returns to the same image from different perspectives.
