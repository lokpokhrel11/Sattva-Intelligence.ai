Concept Index

A growing index of philosophical, ethical, and textual concepts discussed across the Bhagavata Purana series. Entries identify episodes and passages in which a concept receives substantive treatment.

Return to Episode Index

How to Use This Index

The Episode Index follows the Bhagavata Purana chapter by chapter. This Concept Index provides another way into the series. It allows readers, students, and researchers to follow philosophical and ethical questions across different episodes and textual settings.

Entries are included where a concept receives substantive discussion, rather than every time a term happens to appear. Where useful, the index also identifies cross-textual comparison with traditions such as classical Sāṃkhya, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Yoga Sutra.

For Episodes 1–7, links currently lead to the complete episode. From Episode 8 onward, precise timestamps are provided where available. Earlier entries may be given more precise timestamps in the future.

A

Āśraya (आश्रय)

Episode 5 — Canto 2, Chapter 10
Āśraya is the tenth of the Bhagavata Purana's ten characteristics. The episode considers several ways of rendering the term, including support, resort, substratum, and ultimate ground. The first nine characteristics are presented as ways of making this tenth principle intelligible. The episode therefore reads the tenfold list not simply as ten equal topics, but as nine approaches toward an underlying ground.

Aśvamedha (अश्वमेध)

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 12
Yudhishthira's postwar sacrifices, the depleted treasury, and the wealth associated with King Marutta. The passage forms part of the larger movement from war toward restored rule.

Divine Descent (Avatāra / अवतार)

Episode 4 — Canto 2, Chapter 7
The episode examines the avatāras as different forms of divine intervention. Rather than treating the chapter simply as a list, it identifies a recurring pattern: the form of intervention corresponds to the character of the problem. Varāha, Narasiṃha, Matsya, Kapila, Dhanvantari, Vyāsa, and other figures illustrate different forms of protection, teaching, healing, and the preservation of knowledge.

B

Devotion (Bhakti / भक्ति)

Episode 6 — Canto 1, Chapter 2
Bhakti is presented in the discussion of the highest dharma and is qualified as ahaitukī, without ulterior motive, and apratihatā, unobstructed. The episode distinguishes this form of devotion from transactional practice directed toward obtaining a particular reward. It also examines the relationship of bhakti with knowledge and non-attachment.

Watch Episode 6

Episode 7 — Canto 1, Chapters 5–6
Bhakti is developed through Nārada's own life. Repeated hearing in the company of sages gradually changes what he values and how he understands himself. Later, his meditation becomes deeply absorbed in devotion. The episode therefore presents bhakti not only as a principle described by a teacher but as a transformation narrated through a life.

Bhagavata Purana as Light (Purāṇārka / पुराणार्क)

Episode 6 — Canto 1, Chapter 3
The Bhagavata Purana describes itself through the image of a sun arising after Krishna's departure from the world. The episode treats this carefully as a claim the text makes about itself: for people whose vision has become obscured in the age of Kali, the Purana presents itself as a source of illumination.

Brahmā: Creation and Secondary Agency

Episode 3 — Canto 2, Chapters 4–5
Brahmā is presented as a creator operating within a reality whose ultimate source does not lie in himself. The episode distinguishes his extraordinary creative agency from the question of the ultimate source of existence and examines the limits of agency within a larger order.

Watch Episode 3

Episode 4 — Canto 2, Chapter 6
Brahmā places himself within the cosmic order he describes. He is a powerful agent of creation, but not the ultimate source of the materials with which he works. The episode uses this distinction to examine the difference between making, arranging, and originating.

Ten Characteristics of the Bhagavata Purana (Daśa-lakṣaṇa / दश-लक्षण)

Episode 5 — Canto 2, Chapter 10
The Bhagavata Purana pauses its narrative to describe its own subject matter through ten characteristics: Sarga, Visarga, Sthāna, Poṣaṇa, Ūti, Manvantara, Īśānukathā, Nirodha, Mukti, and Āśraya. The episode examines this passage as an instance of textual self-description.

Death / Mortality

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapters 16–19
Parīkṣit learns that he has seven days to live. The episode examines how the knowledge of approaching death reorganizes his priorities and leads him to ask what is worth hearing, knowing, and doing when time is limited.

Knowledge: Completion and Orientation

Episode 7 — Canto 1, Chapter 5
Nārada's criticism of Vyāsa does not arise from a lack of learning or intellectual achievement. Vyāsa has already produced an extraordinary body of knowledge. The episode instead asks what makes knowledge complete and what stands at its center. Knowledge and action are therefore considered not only in terms of excellence or quantity, but also in terms of their orientation and ultimate purpose.

Knowledge Transmission / Trustworthy Sources

Episode 6 — Canto 1, Chapters 1–3
Sūta's authority is connected with what he has studied, whom he has heard, and the teachers from whom the teaching has descended. The episode follows the movement of knowledge through Śuka, Parīkṣit, Sūta, the sages, and later listeners.

Watch Episode 6

Episode 7 — Canto 1, Chapters 5–6
The transmission of knowledge is dramatized through Nārada's own biography. As a child he listens to travelling sages and receives their teaching. Later he becomes the teacher whom Vyāsa hears. The movement from listener to transmitter makes lived reception part of the episode's account of how knowledge passes between generations.

Vyāsa's Dissatisfaction and the Origin of the Bhagavata

Episode 7 — Canto 1, Chapters 4–5
After organizing Vedic knowledge and composing the Mahābhārata, Vyāsa remains dissatisfied. Nārada does not attribute this dissatisfaction to a lack of learning or accomplishment. Instead, he questions the orientation and center of Vyāsa's work. The episode presents this unresolved dissatisfaction as part of the narrative explanation for the composition of the Bhagavata Purana.
