Concept Index

A growing index of philosophical, ethical, and textual concepts discussed across the Bhagavata Purana series. Entries identify episodes and passages in which a concept receives substantive treatment.

Return to Episode Index

How to Use This Index

The Episode Index follows the Bhagavata Purana chapter by chapter. This Concept Index provides another way into the series. It allows readers, students, and researchers to follow philosophical and ethical questions across different episodes and textual settings.

Entries are included where a concept receives substantive discussion, rather than every time a term happens to appear. Where useful, the index also identifies cross-textual comparison with traditions such as classical Sāṃkhya, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Yoga Sutra.

For Episodes 1–7, links currently lead to the complete episode. From Episode 8 onward, precise timestamps are provided where available. Earlier entries may be given more precise timestamps in the future.

A

Aśvamedha (अश्वमेध)

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 12
Yudhishthira's postwar sacrifices, the depleted treasury, and the wealth associated with King Marutta. The passage forms part of the larger movement from war toward restored rule.

D

Death / Mortality

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapters 16–19
Parīkṣit learns that he has seven days to live. The episode examines how the knowledge of approaching death reorganizes his priorities and leads him to ask what is worth hearing, knowing, and doing when time is limited.

Dharma (धर्म)

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapters 16–19
Dharma appears in the form of the bull whose four legs are austerity, purity, compassion, and truth. The episode examines the damage associated with Kali and Parīkṣit's responsibility to restrain wrongdoing and restore what has been damaged.

Watch Episode 1

Episode 2 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
Dharma is considered in relation to judgment and action. The episode asks how one recognizes what a situation requires when mental conditions such as clarity, agitation, or inertia affect perception and response.

G

Three Guṇas (Triguṇa / त्रिगुण)

Episode 2 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
An extended introduction to sattva, rajas, and tamas as three guṇas that shape perception, thought, and action. The episode asks how these different conditions influence judgment and develops the practical question: which one is running me right now?

Watch Episode 2

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 10
Discussion of sattva, rajas, and tamas in relation to Prakṛti and manifestation. The episode distinguishes this primarily cosmological use from later or more familiar uses of the guṇas as descriptions of human disposition.

J

Judgment / Acting Before Understanding

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
Śṛṅgi hears a report about Parīkṣit's conduct, does not verify what happened, and imposes a fatal curse. His father, Śamīka, judges the punishment disproportionate. The episode uses the scene to examine rapid judgment based on incomplete information.

Watch Episode 1

K

Kali Yuga (Kali-yuga / कलियुग)

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapters 16–17
Kali is presented as the spirit of the age. The episode examines Parīkṣit's encounter with Kali and the assigned places associated with gambling, drinking, sexual exploitation, slaughter, and gold.

Watch Episode 1

Name and Form (Nāma-Rūpa / नाम-रूप)

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 10
Name and form in Bhagavata Purana 1.10.22, discussed in relation to Prakṛti and the emergence of a differentiated world.

Parīkṣit (परीक्षित्)

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 12
Parīkṣit's prenatal vision, birth, predicted future, naming, and search for the figure he had seen before birth. The episode connects this beginning of his life with the questions he will later ask as death approaches.

Power and Knowledge

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapter 19
Parīkṣit leaves royal power behind and bows before Śuka. The episode uses this scene to distinguish political authority from knowledge and to examine the willingness to learn from someone outside ordinary structures of status and power.

Prakṛti (प्रकृति)

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 10
Bhagavata Purana 1.10.22 and the relationship between Prakṛti, manifestation, and Krishna's power. The discussion compares the passage with classical Sāṃkhya while preserving the Bhagavata's own conceptual framework.

Presence and Absence

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapters 10–11
Krishna's departure from Hastinapura and return to Dvaraka are examined as two relational perspectives on the same journey: separation for one community and homecoming for another.

Watch from 26:21

Puruṣa (पुरुष)

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 10
Bhagavata Purana 1.10.21 describes Krishna as Puruṣaḥ Purātanaḥ. The episode compares this usage with the Puruṣa–Prakṛti distinction of classical Sāṃkhya. It also explains why the same Sanskrit vocabulary should not automatically be assumed to perform an identical philosophical function across different texts and traditions.

R

Rajas (रजस्)

Episode 2 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
Rajas is examined as movement, passion, striving, and restless activity. The episode treats it as a necessary condition for action while also considering how excessive rajas can produce agitation and distort judgment.

Responsibility

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 10
Yudhishthira returns to rule after Bhishma's death. The discussion considers the movement from responsibility for an irreversible past toward responsibility for action in the present.

S

Sāṃkhya (सांख्य)

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 10
An extended comparison of Puruṣa, Prakṛti, and the three guṇas in classical Sāṃkhya with the related vocabulary of Bhagavata Purana 1.10.21–22. The comparison emphasizes both conceptual overlap and philosophical difference.

Sattva (सत्त्व)

Episode 2 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
Sattva receives sustained treatment as clarity, lucidity, balance, and the capacity to see a situation more accurately. The episode distinguishes sattva from the simpler idea of merely being morally good.

Seeing and Searching

Episode 10 — Canto 1, Chapter 12
Parīkṣit sees the figure who protects him before birth. Bhagavata Purana 1.12.30 later describes him examining or looking among people for the one he had previously seen. The episode places this early search alongside the questions Parīkṣit will ask near the end of his life.

Listening (Śravaṇa / श्रवण)

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapter 19
After learning that he has seven days to live, Parīkṣit gives up his kingdom, goes to the Ganges, and chooses to spend his remaining time listening. Śuka's arrival establishes listening as the setting through which the Bhagavata's teaching will unfold.

Watch Episode 1

T

Tamas (तमस्)

Episode 2 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
Tamas is examined as heaviness, inertia, obscurity, rest, and dissolution. The episode avoids treating tamas as simply evil and considers both its necessary functions and the problems that arise when inertia or obscurity dominates perception and action.

Watch Episode 2

V

Non-attachment (Vairāgya / वैराग्य)

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
Vairāgya is discussed as non-attachment rather than indifference. The episode compares the term with its technical use in the Yoga Sutra and connects it with Parīkṣit's changed relationship to kingdom, wealth, status, and mortality.

Watch Episode 1

Discernment (Viveka / विवेक)

Episode 1 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
Viveka is examined as discriminative discernment, including comparison with its more technical uses in Yoga and Vedanta. The episode applies the concept to Parīkṣit's recognition that mortality changes the relative importance of power, possession, and status.

Watch Episode 1

Episode 2 — Canto 1, Chapters 18–19
Viveka is developed as the capacity to distinguish among mental conditions and recognize which guṇa is presently dominant. The episode connects discernment with the possibility of responding rather than simply being carried by an immediate state.

Watch Episode 2
